O for a muse of bass that would ascend
the brightest ceiling of invention.
A basement for a stage, and dancers all to act,
and strangers to behold the swelling room.
Then should the warlike Olib take the booth,
and at her heels, leashed in like hounds,
the low end, the long night, the morning waiting.
But pardon, all, the flattened spirits that have dared
upon this unworthy floor to bring so great an object forth.
Can this room hold the vasty fields of sound?
May we cram within this wooden O
the very speakers that did shake the air?
O pardon, since a crooked figure may
attest in little place a million.
Let us, ciphers to this great account,
work on your imaginative forces.
Suppose within the girdle of these walls
is now confined one mighty monarchy:
Ampersand,
whose high and upreared front
the perilous narrow street defends.
Olib keeps the Ampersand.
Nobody here keeps the hour.
Piece out our imperfection with your thoughts.
Into a thousand parts divide one crowd
and make imaginary multitudes.
Think, when we talk of speakers, that you hear them,
printing their thunder in the receiving floor.
For it is your bodies now must deck our monarch,
carry them here and there, jumping over hours,
turning the accomplishment of many years into one night.
Sound is all that is the case.
The room is the totality of bodies, not of things.
The limits of my record are the limits of my night.
A record is a picture of reality.
The kick is the general form of the proposition.
What can be shown cannot be said.
Whereof one cannot speak, thereof one must dance.
But not at that tempo.
This is not a drum and bass club.
That is not a preference. It is a definition.
One hundred and seventy four is a number, not an argument.
The room has agreed upon a slower proposition.
A break is not a beat. A beat is not a break.
Some rooms run. This one walks, and arrives first.
Admit me chorus to this history,
who prologue-like your humble patience pray,
gently to hear, kindly to judge, our play.
The night admits no argument.
The room decides.
The floor is the only witness.
Every record ends. That is not an objection.
One club, one street, and the street is not neutral.
Ampersand takes the long way round.
Around it the whole city keeps time.
Nothing here is quiet. Nothing here is still.
And so we begin, at the hour when beginning means nothing,
in a room that has agreed to be somewhere else.
